\begin{abstract}
The design of Joint Communication and Control (JCC) systems is critical for modern cyber-physical applications,
where strict bandwidth constraints often lead to network congestion, packet delays, and severe control instability.
Within this paradigm, developing efficient data compression techniques that maintain control performance is a fundamental 
challenge. To address this, this thesis investigates control-aware comprehensive testbed to validate the proposed model 
and algorithmic designs. 

First, an Event-Triggered Control (ETC) mechanism is implemented to reduce communication frequency in the time domain, 
transmitting sensor data only when the state deviation exceeds a predefined error threshold. Second, to minimize the payload 
of each necessary transmission, a sensitivity-based weighted bit allocation algorithm is developed in the spatial domain.
By leveraging the sensitivity matrix (Hessian) derived from the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR), the algorithm dynamically 
allocates quantization bits based on the physical control-criticality of different robotic joints--for instance, prioritizing 
base joints over end-effectors.

Simulations conducted on the robotic manipulator platform demonstrate the superiority of the proposed framework. 
The co-design of ETC and LQR-weighted compression substantially reduces the overall communication payload while effectively 
mitigating trajectory jittering and preserving near-ideal tracking precision under extreme resource constraints. This research
successfully validates the proposed JCC data compression models, highlighting their immense potential in enhancing the resilience 
and efficiency of resource-constrained networked control systems.
\end{abstract}
